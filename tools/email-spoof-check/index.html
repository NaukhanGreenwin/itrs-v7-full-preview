Most email-security statistics quoted in Canada come from American vendors measuring American domains. These do not. What this check looks at

Three public DNS records protect your domain from email impersonation. SPF lists the servers allowed to send mail for you. DKIM adds a tamper-proof signature to every message. DMARC ties them together and tells receiving servers to block mail that fails — but only when its policy is set to quarantine or reject. Missing or monitor-only records are the gap behind most business email compromise and invoice-redirection fraud.

Free email spoofing check: common questions

Is this email spoofing check free?

Yes. The check is free and ungated: no sign-up, no email address, no credit card and no sales call. It runs in your browser, reads only public DNS records, and returns the result immediately.

Does it work for Canadian business domains?

Yes. It works on any domain, and it was built for Canadian business domains specifically — the benchmark it grades you against is IT Rapid Support’s own measurement of 481 mail-enabled Greater Toronto Area business domains, not a global vendor average. A .ca domain, a .com run by a Canadian company, and a subdomain all work the same way.

What do SPF, DKIM and DMARC actually do?

SPF publishes the list of mail servers allowed to send on your behalf. DKIM adds a cryptographic signature receivers can verify. DMARC ties the two together and tells receiving mail servers what to do when a message fails — nothing, quarantine it, or reject it. Only DMARC at quarantine or reject actually blocks a forged message.

How many Canadian businesses have DMARC set to enforce?

In our own scan of 481 mail-enabled GTA business domains on 1 August 2026, 52.4% published a DMARC record but only 20.6% had it set to quarantine or reject. Another 31.8% sat at p=none, which reports forged mail and blocks none of it. SPF adoption was much higher at 91.7%.

I have a DMARC record. Does that mean my domain is protected?

Not necessarily. A DMARC record set to p=none monitors and reports, and stops nothing. In our GTA measurement, 153 of the 252 domains with DMARC — just over 60% of them — were parked at p=none. This tool reads your policy and tells you which of the three states you are in.

Do you store the domain I check?

No. The lookups run from your browser against public DNS over HTTPS. Nothing is scanned, accessed or probed, no result is written to our systems, and there is no record of what you looked up.

What does it mean if DKIM is not detected?

It means we did not find a key at the common selectors we probe. Some providers use custom selectors that are not discoverable from outside, so a not-detected result is a prompt to confirm rather than proof that DKIM is missing. The same limitation applies to our published research, where the DKIM figure is deliberately reported as an undercount.

What does it cost to fix the gaps this finds?

Publishing SPF, enabling DKIM and moving DMARC past p=none is a short, well-understood piece of configuration work rather than a migration, and it does not require replacing your mail platform.
