What IT Services a Professional Services Firm Actually Needs

Professional services firms are not usually short of technology; they are short of time to manage it. A typical practice runs Microsoft 365 for mail, files and Teams, laptops for staff who move between the office, home and client sites, and one or two specialist applications: CAD and modelling for engineering and architecture, project accounting or time-and-billing for consulting and advisory, document management for anything contractual.

The IT services that matter most follow from that. A 24/7 helpdesk so a fee-earner who cannot open a file is back to work in minutes, not after lunch. Proactive monitoring and patching across every device, including the laptops that rarely come into the office. Microsoft 365 administration with a deliberate structure for client files rather than whatever grew organically. Backups of both devices and Microsoft 365 data, tested by restoring. And layered security in the base agreement rather than as an upgrade.

Specialist applications need a named owner. We manage the infrastructure around them, including servers, licensing, updates, backup and access, and work with the software vendor when a fault sits inside the application itself.

If you were looking for IT professional services in the other sense, meaning one-off project work such as a Microsoft 365 migration, a new network or an office move rather than ongoing support for your firm, our guide to what IT professional services are explains how that work is scoped and how it differs from managed IT.

Client Data, Confidentiality and Security Questionnaires

Firms that work for larger organisations are increasingly asked to prove their security, not just describe it. A client's procurement or risk team sends a questionnaire asking whether MFA is enforced for every user, whether laptops are encrypted, when a backup was last restored, how quickly a departing employee loses access, and what happens if there is an incident. A firm that answers from memory is taking a risk with every answer.

We implement the controls behind those questions and keep a record that they are running, so the questionnaire becomes a matter of reading answers off documentation. The controls that come up most are enforced MFA, device encryption and remote wipe, endpoint detection and response, email authentication with SPF, DKIM and DMARC, monitored and tested backups, audit logging in Microsoft 365, and a written offboarding process. Obligations under PIPEDA and any client contract stay with your firm; our role is the technical controls layer and the evidence for it.

Sharing Files Securely with Clients and Partners

Most data leaks at professional firms are not breaches in the dramatic sense. They are a sharing link that was set to anyone with the link, a former contractor who still has access to a Teams channel, or a large file sent through a personal file-transfer account because the firm's system would not take it. Each is easy to prevent once someone owns the configuration.

We set Microsoft 365 up so external sharing works without becoming a risk: client workspaces separated so one client's documents are not visible to another's guests, sharing links that expire, guest accounts reviewed and removed when an engagement ends, and a sanctioned way to send large drawings, models and data sets so staff do not invent their own. The aim is for the secure route to be the easiest one, because staff will use whatever is fastest when a deadline is close.

Managed IT or Hourly Support for Smaller Practices

A small practice with a few staff, everything in Microsoft 365 and no server can reasonably buy IT support by the hour. We scope each piece of work in writing and bill it by the hour. A flat monthly managed agreement makes more sense once the firm depends on its systems every hour of the working day, carries client confidentiality obligations it has to evidence, or has preventive work that nobody is currently doing.

Professional Services IT FAQs

How do you keep professional services teams productive?

We monitor systems proactively, resolve issues quickly through a responsive helpdesk, and keep Microsoft 365 and collaboration tools reliable so your experts spend their time on billable client work, not IT problems.

How do you protect confidential client information?

We apply encryption, endpoint protection, email security, multi-factor authentication, and managed backups so the sensitive client data your firm handles stays confidential and recoverable.

Can your team support remote and hybrid work?

Yes. We set up secure remote access and Microsoft 365 so your team can work safely from anywhere, with helpdesk support and on-site service across the GTA when needed.

What IT services do professional services firms need most?

Five things carry most of the weight: a helpdesk that resolves problems quickly so fee-earners are not waiting, Microsoft 365 set up with a clear structure for client files and secure external sharing, enforced multi-factor authentication and endpoint protection on every device, backups that are tested by restoring rather than assumed, and a clean process for joiners and leavers. Specialist software such as CAD, project accounting or time-and-billing tools sits on top and needs its own update and backup plan.

Can a small consulting or engineering firm get IT support without a monthly contract?

Yes. One-off and occasional work is scoped in writing before it starts and billed by the hour. That suits a small practice with everything in Microsoft 365 and few problems. If preventive work such as patching, backup testing and offboarding is not getting done, a flat monthly managed agreement is usually the better value, and we will explain why before you decide.

Can you help us answer a client's IT security questionnaire?

Yes. Larger clients increasingly send their suppliers security questionnaires covering MFA, encryption, backup and restore testing, patching, endpoint protection, access reviews and incident response. We implement and document those controls so the answers are accurate and can be backed with evidence, and we help you complete the technical sections. We do not certify your firm as compliant with any standard; the answers and the obligations remain your firm's.
