Managed IT Services for Accounting and CPA Firms in the GTA

An accounting practice is an unusually demanding IT environment for its size. A ten-person firm can hold financial records, banking details, business numbers and personal identity information for several hundred clients, keep years of it for retention reasons, and run it all on a handful of workstations, one server or a cloud tenant, and a mailbox. The value density is closer to a bank's than to a typical small business, and the resourcing rarely reflects that.

IT Rapid Support was founded in 2018 and works with accounting and CPA firms across the Greater Toronto Area from our office at 7810 Keele Street in Vaughan. The service is delivered by one team on one agreement rather than split between a helpdesk vendor and a security vendor, because that split is what reliably produces the gap neither side owns when something goes wrong. Support, security, backup and Microsoft 365 administration sit inside a single fixed monthly fee scoped to the users and devices we cover.

What that covers day to day is deliberately ordinary: a 24/7 helpdesk staff can reach, proactive monitoring and patching, management of the network and storage your tax and accounting applications depend on, Microsoft 365 and Azure administration handled in-house, and monitored encrypted backups with tested restores. We manage the infrastructure around your tax, bookkeeping and practice software rather than the applications themselves, and we deal with those vendors directly when an issue belongs to them.

Tax Season Is an Availability Problem, Not a Software Problem

Firms usually describe their busy-season risk in terms of software, and it is almost never the software. What actually costs a firm hours in the last fortnight before a deadline is a workstation that will not boot, a server disk that filled with nobody watching, a network that slows to a crawl once every seat is in use at once, a Microsoft 365 account locked out at the worst possible moment, or an upgrade that someone approved in February and that landed in the third week of April.

So the work is scheduling as much as it is technology. Hardware replacement, operating system upgrades, storage expansion and any migration get planned well outside the filing peaks, and a change freeze on non-urgent work through the crunch is the normal arrangement rather than a special request. Monitoring runs continuously so that capacity, failing drives and backup failures surface weeks ahead instead of on the morning they matter. Where remote access is part of how the firm works through the peak, it is set up and tested before the volume arrives, not during it.

The 24/7 helpdesk matters more in that fortnight than in the rest of the year combined, because the hours a firm works in a deadline week are not business hours. We do not publish a guaranteed response time, and we would rather say that plainly than quote a number we cannot hold to. What we do commit to is that the queue is staffed outside business hours and that the phone is answered by a person.

Protecting Client Financial Data, and the Attack That Actually Arrives

The breach that reaches an accounting firm is rarely sophisticated. It is a phishing email that harvests a Microsoft 365 password, a mailbox entered because multi-factor authentication was never enforced, ransomware arriving through an unpatched workstation, or an invoice quietly altered in a compromised email thread. The controls that stop most of it are unremarkable and are the ones firms most often have partially in place: multi-factor authentication on every account without exception, managed endpoint protection, email filtering that catches phishing before it reaches a person, disciplined patching, access controls so staff reach only the client files their role requires, and managed detection and response watching around the clock.

Email authentication deserves its own mention, because impersonation of a firm to its own clients is the fraud that does the commercial damage. We configure SPF, DKIM and DMARC on your domain and move DMARC to a policy that rejects forged mail rather than leaving it at p=none, which reports impersonation and blocks none of it. Our own measurement of the public DNS records of 481 mail-enabled Greater Toronto Area business domains found 91.7% publishing SPF and only 20.6% enforcing DMARC, and the narrower study of 118 GTA business domains found 40% fully protected. You can check your own domain in about ten seconds with our free email spoofing check.

Backups are the last line and the one most often trusted on faith. We monitor them rather than assume them and test restores rather than read status emails, because the only meaningful question about a backup is whether the data came back, and the day you find out should not be the day you need it.

CRA Access, Client Files and PIPEDA: Handling the Data Properly

Accounting firms hold the personal information PIPEDA was written about: social insurance numbers, income, banking details and identity documents, collected for one purpose and expected to be protected accordingly. PIPEDA requires safeguards appropriate to the sensitivity of the information and, since 2018, records of breaches of security safeguards and reporting of those that create a real risk of significant harm. The obligation is the firm's; the technical safeguards are where we come in. Our PIPEDA compliance IT checklist lists them in plain terms.

Two parts of an accounting practice deserve particular care. The first is access to the Canada Revenue Agency's online services for representatives: those credentials and the multi-factor authentication behind them belong to named individuals, should never be shared between staff, and should be removed promptly when someone leaves, which is an identity-management task as much as a policy one. The second is how files move between the firm and its clients. Tax slips and statements sent as email attachments sit in two mailboxes indefinitely; a client portal or managed sharing link with expiry, logging and staff-side multi-factor authentication is the better pattern.

Underneath both sits ordinary discipline: staff reach only the client files their role requires, laptops are encrypted and managed so a lost device is not a disclosure, retention settings follow a decision the firm has made about how long records are kept, and logging means the question of who opened a file has an answer. For a self-check your partners can work through, see our IT and cybersecurity checklist for accounting firms.

Hosting Tax, Bookkeeping and Practice Software

Most firms run a mix: tax preparation software licensed per seat, a working-paper or engagement tool, a bookkeeping platform that may already be cloud-hosted by its vendor, a practice management or time-and-billing system, and Microsoft 365 holding everything else. Each has its own rules about where it can run, how it is licensed and what it needs from the network and storage beneath it.

We assess what you run and check each vendor's supported configurations before recommending anything. Some applications run well on a managed office server; some on an Azure virtual machine with secure remote access, which suits firms whose staff work from home through the peak; and some are best left with the vendor's own hosting. The aim is the arrangement that stays available in a deadline week and restores cleanly after a failure, and annual software updates are scheduled and tested before the filing season rather than installed in the middle of it.

Accounting Firms IT FAQs

How do you keep accounting firms running during tax season?

We monitor your systems proactively to catch issues before they cause downtime, provide a responsive helpdesk that scales for peak filing periods, and maintain tested backups so a failure never costs you a deadline.

How is our clients’ financial data protected?

We apply encryption, endpoint protection, email security, multi-factor authentication, and managed backups, and help your firm align with PIPEDA so sensitive financial information stays confidential.

Can your team support QuickBooks, tax software, and Microsoft 365?

Yes. We manage the infrastructure, security, and Microsoft 365 environment around your accounting and tax applications and provide helpdesk support to your staff so everything stays fast and available.

What does managed IT for an accounting firm actually include?

A 24/7 helpdesk your staff can reach, proactive monitoring and patching of workstations and servers, management of the network and storage your tax and accounting applications depend on, Microsoft 365 and Azure administration handled in-house, multi-factor authentication, managed endpoint protection, email security with SPF, DKIM and DMARC configured on your domain, managed detection and response, and monitored encrypted backups with tested restores. It is billed as a fixed monthly fee scoped to the users and devices covered, so preventive work is part of the service rather than a separate invoice.

Do you help accounting firms work toward PIPEDA and CPA Ontario expectations?

We supply and document the technical controls those obligations rest on: access control so staff reach only the client files their role requires, encryption in transit and at rest where the platform supports it, multi-factor authentication on every account, logging, retention settings configured to a decision the firm has actually made, and recoverable backups. Compliance itself is broader than technology and stays with the firm, including policy, engagement practice, staff training and professional judgment. We handle the technology layer properly and say plainly where it stops.

How do you handle the pre-deadline crunch without us hiring more IT?

Mostly by moving the work earlier. Hardware replacement, operating system upgrades, storage expansion and any migration are scheduled well before a filing period rather than during one, and a change freeze on non-urgent work through the peak weeks is normal practice. Monitoring runs continuously so capacity and failing disks surface in advance, and the helpdesk is available around the clock, which matters more in the last week of a deadline than at any other time of year.

Is a small CPA practice too small for managed IT?

No, and small practices are often the more exposed ones: no in-house IT, a single machine or server holding years of client financial records, and a mailbox that has never had multi-factor authentication enabled. The scope is sized to what you run rather than to a minimum seat count, and the fixed monthly fee is what makes patching, monitoring and backup verification worth doing at that size.

Do you provide IT support for CPA firms outside the GTA?

Yes, remotely. Helpdesk, monitoring, patching, Microsoft 365 administration, security and backup management are delivered remotely and are available to accounting and CPA firms across Canada. On-site work, such as cabling, network hardware or a workstation that will not start, is dispatched from our Vaughan office and covers Toronto and the Greater Toronto Area.

How should our staff exchange tax documents with clients?

Not as email attachments. Slips, statements and returns carry social insurance numbers, banking details and income information, and an attachment sits in two mailboxes indefinitely. A client portal or a managed file-sharing link with expiry, access logging and multi-factor authentication on the staff side is the safer pattern. We set up and secure the sharing platform your firm chooses, including Microsoft 365 options, and restrict external sharing to it.

Can you host our tax and accounting software in the cloud?

Often, yes, though it depends on how each application is licensed and supported. Some products are already cloud-hosted by their vendor, some run well on a managed server or Azure virtual machine with secure remote access, and some are simplest left on a well-maintained office server. We assess what you run, check each vendor's supported configurations, and recommend the arrangement that keeps it available in a deadline week rather than the one that is most fashionable.

What happens to our client data if we are hit by ransomware?

That depends almost entirely on whether the backups restore, which is why we monitor them rather than assume them and test restores rather than trust a green checkmark. With monitored local and cloud copies in place, an incident becomes a recovery exercise with a known cost in hours. The work before that point is what reduces the odds of getting there: multi-factor authentication everywhere, managed endpoint protection, email filtering, disciplined patching, and detection and response watching around the clock.
