Regulator and Insurer Security Expectations, in Plain Terms

The security rules a financial firm answers to depend on what kind of firm it is, and it is worth being precise. OSFI guidelines such as B-13 on technology and cyber risk apply to federally regulated financial institutions: banks, federally regulated insurers and trust and loan companies. They do not apply directly to an independent advisory practice or a mortgage brokerage, but firms that work with those institutions often receive security questions shaped by them.

Investment dealers and mutual fund dealers are regulated by CIRO, whose rules include cybersecurity expectations for dealer members, including reporting of cybersecurity incidents. Firms that are reporting entities under Canada's anti-money-laundering law also have record-keeping obligations with FINTRAC. Which of these applies, and how, is a compliance question for the firm and its compliance officer. Our job is the technology underneath: controls that are actually in place, logs and records that are retained, and evidence that can be produced when an examiner, a dealer or an insurer asks. For a step-by-step self-review, our IT support checklist for financial services firms covers accounts and MFA, wire-fraud controls, devices, record retention, backups and incident response.

Cyber insurers ask the most practical questions of all: enforced MFA, tested and separated backups, endpoint detection and response, email filtering, and who is responsible. Our cyber insurance readiness checklist lists the controls behind those answers.

Email Fraud and Wire-Fraud Controls

The attack that costs financial firms the most money is usually not malware. It is an email, in a real thread or from a lookalike domain, asking for a transfer, a change of banking details or a redemption to a new account. Controls work in layers. SPF, DKIM and DMARC on your own domain, with DMARC moved to an enforcing policy, stop criminals sending as you to your clients; our SPF, DKIM and DMARC guide explains the setup, and the free email spoofing check shows where your domain stands in seconds.

Inside the tenant: multi-factor authentication on every mailbox, alerts on new forwarding and inbox rules, external-sender tagging, and filtering tuned for impersonation of your principals. The control that matters most is a procedure rather than a setting: any request to move money or change payment instructions is confirmed by calling a number already on file, never one supplied in the email. We help firms write that procedure down and make it routine for staff.

Client Data Under PIPEDA

Most private-sector financial firms in Ontario handle client personal information under PIPEDA. In practical terms that means appropriate safeguards for the sensitivity of the data, an individual designated as accountable for compliance, and, since 2018, reporting breaches of security safeguards that create a real risk of significant harm to the Privacy Commissioner of Canada, notifying affected clients, and keeping a record of every breach.

Technology supports that through access controls so staff reach only the client files their role needs, encryption on laptops and phones, retention settings that match the firm's own record-keeping decisions, monitored backups, and logging that shows who accessed what. PIPEDA compliance is the firm's responsibility and includes policy and training beyond IT; our PIPEDA compliance IT checklist covers the technical side.

Financial Services IT FAQs

Do you help financial firms meet compliance requirements?

We implement security controls, access management, logging, encryption, and managed backups that help your firm work toward the standards it is held to, including PCI-DSS and PIPEDA, and document your security posture.

How is sensitive financial data protected?

We use a multi-layered approach: encryption, endpoint protection, email security, multi-factor authentication, managed detection and response, and tested backups so client financial data stays protected and recoverable.

Can you provide reliable, always-on support?

Yes. Our 24/7 helpdesk and proactive monitoring keep your systems available, and we provide on-site support across the GTA when an issue needs in-person attention.

What does IT support for financial services firms include?

A helpdesk for advisors and staff, Microsoft 365 and email administration, management of the devices and network the firm runs on, monitored backups with tested restores, and the security controls client financial data needs: enforced MFA, endpoint protection, email authentication and fraud controls, access reviews and logging. We support the infrastructure around your planning, portfolio and CRM tools and coordinate with those vendors rather than replacing them.

Do OSFI cyber guidelines apply to a small advisory or brokerage firm?

Not directly. OSFI guidelines such as B-13 apply to federally regulated financial institutions like banks and federally regulated insurers. Smaller firms answer to their own regulators and to privacy law, and often receive security questionnaires from the institutions they work with. Confirm your specific obligations with your compliance officer; we implement and document the technical controls.

How do you reduce the risk of wire and payment fraud by email?

By layering controls: SPF, DKIM and an enforcing DMARC policy so your domain cannot be spoofed to clients, MFA on every mailbox, alerts on new forwarding and inbox rules, impersonation filtering, and a written call-back procedure that confirms any payment or banking change on a number already on file.
