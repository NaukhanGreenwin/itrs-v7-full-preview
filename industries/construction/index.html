A contractor runs a head office, a rotating set of job sites and trailers, a fleet of vehicles, and a crew whose primary device is a phone. Choosing IT Support Services for a Construction or Trades Company

Most contractors who call us are not short of IT products; they are short of someone accountable for them. The usual starting point is a mix of a part-time helper, a reseller who sold the laptops, a phone carrier who set up the email and nobody who knows who holds the administrator passwords. Before comparing prices, write down who currently answers the phone when a site laptop will not connect at 6:30 in the morning, who owns the Microsoft 365 tenant and the domain, and when backups were last restored rather than just reported as successful.

Then compare providers on the things that actually differ in construction work: whether the helpdesk is reachable before office hours when crews start, whether phones and tablets are managed and can be wiped remotely, whether site and trailer connections are designed around the address rather than promised, and whether the monthly fee covers security and backup or treats them as add-ons. Our longer guide to IT support for construction and trades in Ontario works through those questions in more detail, and our guide to comparing managed IT quotes shows what should be written into the agreement.

Job Sites, Trailers and Connectivity That Actually Works

Site connectivity is the request we get most often and the one with the least honest marketing around it. What is achievable at a given site is decided almost entirely by the address. Some locations have a usable wired service available and a lead time measured in weeks. Many have nothing, and the realistic answer is a cellular-based connection with the bandwidth and reliability that implies. Pretending otherwise produces a trailer that cannot upload a set of drawings on the morning it matters.

So the design starts from the connection the site can actually get, and then makes the rest tolerant of it: files synchronised from managed cloud storage rather than opened live over a marginal link, large drawing sets cached locally, and applications chosen and configured on the assumption that the connection will drop rather than that it will not. The trailer is set up so it can be struck and moved to the next job without being rebuilt, because it will be.

The part that gets skipped is that the site is on the same accounts and the same security standard as head office. A trailer running a consumer router on a flat network with a shared password is a route into the company, and it is a route that is physically accessible to anyone on site. Segmentation, managed devices, multi-factor authentication and endpoint protection apply to the trailer exactly as they apply to the office.

Mobile Crews, Site Devices and the Ones That Do Not Come Back

The device that matters most in construction is the one in a pocket on a site, and it is the device most likely to be dropped, stolen, left in a truck or replaced without telling anyone. The difference between that being a nuisance and being a disclosure is whether the device was managed before it went missing.

Managed means the company can revoke access and wipe the device remotely, the data on it was encrypted, and the files were synchronised from a managed location rather than existing only on that phone. It also means the account behind it has multi-factor authentication enforced, so a stolen device is not a stolen mailbox. None of that is possible on a personal device that was quietly added to a mailbox two years ago, which is why the inventory work at the start of an engagement is usually the most revealing part of it.

The same applies to people rather than hardware. Construction has genuine turnover, subcontractors, and seasonal crews, and accounts created for people who left last season are one of the most common findings when we take an environment over. Prompt disabling of accounts when someone leaves is both a housekeeping task and a security control, and it is one of the fifteen areas scored by our free IT risk calculator, which runs in your browser and sends nothing back to us.

Project Data, Bids and the Invoice Fraud Aimed at Contractors

Drawings, revisions, submittals, bids, change orders, photographs and daily reports are the record of the job, and in a dispute they are the entire argument. Losing them is not an IT inconvenience, it is a commercial exposure that can outlast the project by years. Managed cloud storage with real permissions, retention configured to a decision the company has made, version history, and monitored backups with tested restores are what turn that record into something you can rely on rather than something you hope is on somebody's laptop.

The attack that reaches contractors most often is financial rather than technical: an email in an existing thread, apparently from a supplier or a subcontractor, with changed banking details on an invoice, or an impersonation of the company sent to a client. The technical defence is email authentication done properly. We configure SPF, DKIM and DMARC on your domain and move DMARC to a policy that rejects forged mail rather than leaving it at p=none. Our own measurement of 481 mail-enabled Greater Toronto Area business domains found 91.7% publishing SPF and only 20.6% enforcing DMARC, and our narrower study of 118 GTA business domains found 40% fully protected. Check your own domain in about ten seconds with our free email spoofing check.

The procedural half is not something a provider can sell you, so we will simply say it: any change to banking details gets verified by a phone call to a number you already held, before payment. That single habit catches the fraud that the best-configured mail system in the world will still occasionally let through.

Drawings and Project Files in SharePoint and OneDrive

Most contractors we meet keep drawings in three places at once: a server or NAS at head office, a personal cloud account someone set up years ago, and email attachments going back and forth with consultants. That is how a crew ends up building from a superseded revision. Microsoft 365, which most firms already pay for, can replace all three if it is set up for the way projects actually run.

We build one SharePoint site or library per project, with permissions assigned to groups such as estimating, project management, site supervisors and accounting, so access follows the role rather than whoever asked last. Subcontractors and consultants are invited as external guests to the specific folders they need, with expiry dates on the access. Version history means a replaced drawing can be recovered, and OneDrive sync keeps the current set available on a laptop in a trailer when the connection drops. When the job closes, the library is set to read-only and kept for the retention period you choose.

Estimating, Accounting and Project Management Software

Construction companies run on a few specialist applications: estimating and takeoff tools, construction accounting and payroll, and project management or scheduling software, some installed on a local server and some hosted by the vendor. Those systems hold the bids, job costs and payroll records the business depends on, and they are often the least documented part of the environment.

Our part is everything around the application. We manage the user accounts and permissions, keep the machines and any server it runs on patched, schedule installs and version upgrades outside working hours, track the licences so a renewal does not lapse, enforce multi-factor authentication where the product supports it, and back up its data with restores we actually test. For problems inside the software itself, we open and follow the ticket with the vendor's support team on your behalf, so your office manager is not the one relaying messages between two support desks.

Opening and Closing a Job Site: The IT Checklist

Most of the IT trouble on a project comes from its first and last weeks. Before mobilization, order the site connection with enough lead time for what the address can actually get, decide which managed devices go to site, create the project's SharePoint library and permissions, and set up accounts for new hires and subcontractors so nobody shares a login on day one.

At demobilization, the steps are the reverse and are the ones most often skipped. Collect and reassign the laptops, tablets and phones, and wipe any that are not coming back. Disable the accounts and guest access for people who were only there for that job. Set the project files to read-only and confirm they are in the backup, then cancel the site internet service and return the equipment so the company is not paying for a connection on an empty lot. We run both lists as part of construction IT support so neither depends on someone remembering.

Head Office IT for Contractors: Plotters, Scanners and the Shared Drive

Every contractor's head office has the same handful of devices that cause most of the support calls: a large-format plotter, a scanner for drawings and signed documents, a multifunction printer, and an old file server or network storage box holding twenty years of jobs. They are easy to ignore until the plotter stops printing the morning a set has to go out.

Construction & Trades IT FAQs

How do you connect job sites to head office?

We set up reliable cloud collaboration, Microsoft 365, and secure remote access so crews on site, project managers, and the office can share files and communicate seamlessly, with helpdesk support when issues arise.

Can you secure devices used in the field?

Yes. We apply endpoint protection, multi-factor authentication, and secure access controls to the laptops, tablets, and phones your teams use on site so mobile work stays protected.

How is our project data protected?

We provide managed cloud storage and tested backups for drawings, bids, and project records, protected by encryption and security controls so critical files are safe from loss or ransomware.

What does managed IT for a construction company actually include?

A 24/7 helpdesk your site and office staff can reach, proactive monitoring and patching of the machines and servers you run, Microsoft 365 and Azure administration, mobile device management for the laptops, tablets and phones that leave the building, multi-factor authentication, managed endpoint protection, email security with SPF, DKIM and DMARC on your domain, managed detection and response, and monitored encrypted backups with tested restores. It is a fixed monthly fee scoped to the users and devices covered, and on-site work is dispatched from our office at 7810 Keele Street in Vaughan.

Can you set up connectivity for a new site trailer?

Yes, and it is worth planning a few weeks before the trailer lands rather than the week it does. What is achievable depends entirely on the address: some sites have a usable wired service available, many do not, and the honest answer on a greenfield site is often a cellular-based connection with the constraints that implies. We design around the connection the site can actually get, keep the site and head office on the same accounts and the same security standard, and set the trailer up so it can be taken down and moved to the next job without rebuilding it.

How do you protect us from the fake-invoice and payment-redirection emails?

Two layers. Technically, we configure SPF, DKIM and DMARC on your domain and move DMARC to a policy that rejects forged mail rather than leaving it at p=none, which reports impersonation and stops none of it, and we filter inbound phishing before it reaches a person. Our measurement of 481 mail-enabled Greater Toronto Area business domains found 91.7% publishing SPF and only 20.6% enforcing DMARC, so most companies that assume this is handled are in the reporting-only state. Procedurally, the control that actually catches it is verifying any change to banking details by phone on a number you already held, and we will say so rather than sell you a product instead.

What happens when a phone or laptop is lost on a job site?

If the device is managed, it is a replacement cost rather than a disclosure: access is revoked, the device can be wiped remotely, and the data on it was encrypted and synchronised from a managed location rather than living only on that machine. If it is an unmanaged personal device holding company files, none of that is true, which is why device management is part of the service rather than an upgrade.

Do you support the record-keeping our reporting obligations depend on?

Indirectly but importantly. Obligations such as WSIB reporting, health and safety documentation, contract records and warranty evidence all assume the underlying records are available and recoverable years later. We handle that technology layer: managed storage rather than files scattered across personal drives, retention configured to a decision the company has actually made, and monitored backups with tested restores. What must be recorded and reported, and by when, remains the company's responsibility.

What should a construction company look for in IT support services?

Four things: a helpdesk that site staff can reach outside office hours, device management for the phones, tablets and laptops that leave the building, someone who will plan job-site and trailer connectivity around what the address can actually get, and backups of drawings, bids and project records that are tested by restoring them. Ask any provider how each of those is handled and what is included in the monthly fee rather than billed as extra.

The core is the same: helpdesk, monitoring and patching, Microsoft 365, security and tested backups.
