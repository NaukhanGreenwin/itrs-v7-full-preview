What We Have Actually Measured About GTA Business IT

Most IT support pages tell you the provider is trusted and experienced. We would rather show our work. Since mid-2026 we have been running our own original research on a fixed sample of real GTA business domains — the same sample re-scanned each time, so the numbers can be compared across studies — and publishing the results in full, including the findings that surprised us.

Our GTA small-business cybersecurity report measured email authentication across 481 GTA business domains: SPF adoption is high at 91.7%, but only 20.6% of domains enforce DMARC — meaning the large majority of GTA businesses still leave their domain open to convincing impersonation. Our GTA email platform study found 46.8% of mail-enabled GTA business domains run Microsoft 365 and 25.1% run Google Workspace, with sharply different security postures between the two populations. And our GTA website security study of 470 business websites found 45.5% send zero of the five basic security headers and 18.3% do not even force HTTPS.

The method started smaller. Our first study reviewed the public DNS records of 118 GTA business domains and found only 40% fully protected against email spoofing. That result is what led us to build the free email spoofing check, which runs the same lookups against your own domain and tells you where you sit in that distribution.

We publish these because they are the honest baseline for the region. When we say a security control is unusual or overdue for a GTA business, it is because we measured the region — not because a vendor brochure said so. How we act on it is set out on our pages for cybersecurity services across the GTA and IT security services in York Region.

One Team for the Helpdesk and the Security Work

The helpdesk is where security problems show up first: a user reporting a suspicious email, repeated account lockouts, an odd Microsoft 365 sign-in prompt, a failed backup notification. When support and security are two different vendors, those early signals become handoffs. When they are one team, the person who takes the call can also check the sign-in logs, isolate the endpoint, and escalate to managed detection and response without a second contract getting in the way.

That is why the security baseline is built into our managed service rather than sold as add-ons: multi-factor authentication, endpoint protection, patching, secure Microsoft 365 configuration, SPF, DKIM and DMARC on your domain, and monitored backups. Our managed security services page explains the full stack, and the IT risk calculator will show you where your current setup stands before you talk to anyone. Signs a GTA Business Has Outgrown Ad-Hoc IT Support

Plenty of GTA businesses start with a relative, a staff member who is good with computers, or a technician called when something breaks. That works until it does not, and the signs are usually visible well before a serious outage: the same problems come back every few weeks, nobody can say with certainty that last night's backup worked, admin passwords live in one person's head, laptops have not been patched in months, and a new hire waits days for a working account.

A second set of signs comes from outside: an insurer's cyber questionnaire asks about multi-factor authentication and endpoint detection and you are not sure of the answer, a client asks how their data is protected, or you open a second location and realise nothing is standardised. At that point IT support services stop being a convenience and become the way the business keeps running. Our security self-check takes a few minutes and shows where the gaps are.

What to Have Ready Before You Call an IT Support Services Company

A useful first conversation with any IT support services company in the GTA takes less time when a few facts are to hand. You do not need all of them; a rough answer is fine, and finding the gaps is part of the work.

  • How many staff use a computer, and roughly how many laptops, desktops, phones and servers you have.
  • Every location you work from across the GTA, plus how many people work from home.
  • Whether you run Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace, and who holds the administrator login today.
  • Where your domain is registered and who can change its DNS records.
  • Your internet provider at each site and who manages the firewall and Wi-Fi.
  • What is backed up, where to, and when a restore was last tested.
  • Any line-of-business software, client requirements or insurance questions you have to answer.

With that list, the scope and quote can be put in writing quickly, whether the work ends up billed by the hour or under a monthly plan.

The First Weeks of IT Support Services: What Onboarding Covers

The first weeks with a new provider decide whether the rest of the relationship goes well. A careful onboarding documents the environment before changing it: every user, device, licence, network device and vendor contact is recorded, and administrator access to Microsoft 365, the domain and the firewall is confirmed and held in your business's name, not the provider's.

Monitoring and patching are installed next, backups are checked with a real test restore, and the security baseline (multi-factor authentication, endpoint protection and email authentication) is brought up to standard. Staff get one number and one email address for the helpdesk. If you are leaving another provider, our guide on how to switch IT providers covers the handover in order.

GTA IT Support FAQs

How much does IT support cost in the GTA?

We do not publish a one-size-fits-all rate card, because a quoted price without scope is not comparable to anything. GTA managed IT pricing is a fixed monthly fee set by user and device count, the security tier included, server and cloud infrastructure, and whether after-hours coverage and on-site work are in scope. Our managed IT plans page lays out exactly what each tier includes, line by line, so you can compare it against any other quote.

Can you work alongside our internal IT team?

Yes. Co-managed IT is a standard arrangement for larger GTA organizations: your internal team keeps the day-to-day knowledge and priorities, and we add 24/7 helpdesk coverage, security tooling and monitoring, patching, backup oversight, and specialist depth for projects. The split of responsibilities is written down so nothing falls between the two teams.

How do we switch IT providers without downtime?

A proper transition runs in parallel: we document your environment, take over monitoring and backups, verify we hold the Microsoft 365 tenant access, domain records, passwords, and vendor contacts, and only then does the old provider step away. Staff keep working throughout — the helpdesk number changes, their tools do not. Before signing with anyone, confirm in writing who owns your tenant, credentials, and documentation if you leave.

Which areas of the GTA do you provide IT support in?

We provide managed IT support and cybersecurity services across the entire Greater Toronto Area from our Vaughan headquarters — including Toronto, Mississauga, Brampton, Markham, Vaughan, Richmond Hill, Oakville, Burlington, Hamilton, and the surrounding York, Peel, Halton, and Durham regions. Remote support is immediate, and certified technicians provide scheduled or emergency on-site support GTA-wide.

What IT support services do you offer GTA businesses?

We act as a complete IT department for GTA businesses: 24/7 helpdesk, proactive monitoring and maintenance, managed cybersecurity with detection and response, cloud and Microsoft 365 management, network management, data backup and disaster recovery, vCIO strategy, and on-site support when an issue is best resolved in person.

How fast can you respond to IT issues in the GTA?

Remote support starts immediately — our 24/7 helpdesk answers around the clock, every day of the year. For issues that need hands-on work, we dispatch certified technicians on-site across the GTA, with our Vaughan head office centrally located to reach Toronto, York Region, Peel, and beyond quickly.

Do you support businesses with multiple offices across the GTA?

Yes. Many GTA businesses operate from more than one location. We manage multi-site networks, standardize security across offices, and support hybrid and remote staff, so every location gets the same level of IT support and protection.

What do IT support services in the GTA include?

Our IT support services in the GTA include a 24/7 helpdesk, monitoring and patching of computers, servers and network devices, Microsoft 365 administration, managed security such as multi-factor authentication and endpoint protection, monitored backups, network and Wi-Fi management, and on-site visits when the fix is physical.

Can we get IT support in the GTA without a monthly contract?

Yes. General IT work is available by the hour, which suits one-off projects and occasional problems. Businesses that want monitoring, security and the helpdesk covered for a fixed fee can choose one of our monthly managed IT plans instead.

Is your GTA IT support remote or on site?

Both, remote first. Most issues are fixed on the call by connecting to the device, wherever you are in the GTA. When hands-on work is needed, such as hardware, cabling, Wi-Fi or an office move, a visit is scheduled from our office at 7810 Keele St in Vaughan.

It depends on how many users, devices and locations you have and how much of your current setup is already documented. Do IT support services in the GTA cover staff who work from home?

Yes. Remote support works wherever the laptop has an internet connection, so hybrid and home-based staff anywhere in the GTA, or outside it, get the same helpdesk, patching, endpoint protection and multi-factor authentication as people in the office.
