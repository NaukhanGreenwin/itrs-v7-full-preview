Managed IT in Markham starts with who already does the IT

Most Markham businesses that call us are not starting from nothing. In a city with this many technology employers, there is usually someone in the office who handles IT: a developer who set up the Microsoft 365 tenant in an afternoon, an operations manager who owns the firewall password, or a full-time IT coordinator who is good at the job and has no one to cover their holidays. The first question in a Markham managed IT conversation is therefore not which plan you want but what that person should keep and what they should hand over.

Our agreement has the same components wherever we work: a 24/7 helpdesk where a technician answers, monitoring and patching of servers, workstations and network devices, Microsoft 365 and cloud administration, monitored backups with test restores, and the security layer. What changes in Markham is how those pieces are divided. A fully managed client hands us all of it. A co-managed client keeps the parts that benefit from someone in the building and passes us the parts that need depth, tooling or coverage at 2 am. Both are written down so nobody assumes the other side is watching the backups.

Software, engineering and tech firms along the 404 and 407 corridor

The office parks around Woodbine Avenue, Allstate Parkway and Cochrane Drive, close to the Highway 404 and 407 interchange, hold a large share of Markham's software, hardware and engineering companies. Their IT problems look different from a typical small office. The engineers can run their own build servers and cloud accounts, and they usually want to. What goes unowned is everything around the product: who controls the Microsoft 365 global admin account, whether a departing developer's laptop was actually wiped, whether the Git and cloud consoles enforce multi-factor sign-in, and who answers when a sales director is locked out the night before a customer demo.

That is the split we normally propose. Your developers keep the product stack, the code repositories and the CI pipelines. We take identity, devices, email security, backup and the helpdesk. Onboarding and offboarding become a checklist we run every time rather than a favour someone remembers to do, which matters for teams that hire in bursts after a funding round or a large contract.

The other pressure comes from customers. A bank, insurer, hospital or public-sector buyer will send a Markham software vendor a security questionnaire that asks about MFA, endpoint protection, encryption, backup testing and incident response. We help you answer it accurately, and where the honest answer is not yet yes, we fix the control first. The York Region version of that security work is described on our cybersecurity services in York Region page.

Professional and healthcare practices in Unionville, Main Street and Markham Centre

Away from the corridor, Markham's other business base is the professional practice: accounting firms, law offices, dental and medical clinics, physiotherapists, insurance and mortgage brokers, many of them in converted houses and small plazas along Main Street Unionville and Main Street Markham, or in the newer mid-rise buildings growing up around Markham Centre and Cornell. These offices tend to have ten to forty people, one practice-management or EMR system everything depends on, and nobody whose job is IT.

For them, managed IT is mostly about reliability and confidentiality. The practice system has to be up when the first patient or client arrives, the workstations at reception have to be patched without interrupting appointments, and client files and health records have to be encrypted, access-controlled and backed up. Accounting firms add a hard seasonal peak: a server or Microsoft 365 problem in late April costs far more than the same problem in August, so maintenance and upgrades are scheduled around tax season rather than into it.

Health practices hold personal health information under PHIPA, and most other practices hold personal information under PIPEDA. Our controls (individual logins, MFA, encrypted devices, audited access, tested backups) support those obligations, but they do not satisfy them by themselves; the policies, consents and privacy officer remain the practice's responsibility, and we say so plainly. Our dental office IT guide and PIPEDA IT checklist go into the detail.

Multilingual teams, overseas partners and Microsoft 365

Many Markham offices work in more than one language, and many work with suppliers, partners, development teams or head offices in other time zones. That has practical IT consequences that a generic setup misses. Staff sign in from overseas when they travel to visit family or partners, so a blanket block on foreign sign-ins locks out legitimate users, while no restriction at all leaves the door open. We use conditional access with named exceptions and short expiry windows instead, so travel is planned for rather than discovered at the airport.

Phishing arrives in every language your staff read, including invoice and shipping lures written in Chinese, Korean, Tamil or Farsi that English-tuned training never shows them. Mail filtering, SPF, DKIM and DMARC on your own domain, and short, plain-language training that uses real examples are the controls that help here. Mailbox language, regional settings and keyboard layouts are configured per user rather than left for each person to fix on their own. Our managed Microsoft 365 services page covers the tenant side in more depth.

Co-managed IT for Markham firms with an in-house IT person

If you already employ an IT person, a co-managed agreement is usually a better fit than replacing them. The typical division in Markham looks like this: your person keeps user relationships, projects, vendor decisions and anything that needs a face in the office; we provide the 24/7 helpdesk behind them, monitoring and patch management, endpoint detection and response, backup monitoring and restore testing, and a team to lean on during an incident or a holiday.

We share the same ticketing and documentation, so your person can see what we did overnight and we can see what they changed during the day. The arrangement is described on our co-managed IT services page, and our guide to co-managed versus fully managed IT helps decide which model suits your firm today. Many clients start co-managed and move to fully managed when their internal person changes roles, or the other way round when a growing firm hires its first IT lead.

Endpoints, backup and the controls that decide a bad week

Every managed device is enrolled, encrypted and covered by endpoint detection and response, with operating systems and common applications patched on a schedule set around your working hours. Devices that fall behind are reported by name rather than averaged into a percentage. Lost or stolen laptops can be locked and wiped, which matters for consultants and engineers who carry client data between offices.

Backups cover Microsoft 365 mail, OneDrive, SharePoint and Teams separately from Microsoft's own retention, plus any server or NAS still in the office. They are monitored daily and restore-tested on a schedule, because a backup that has never been restored is an assumption rather than a plan. When a ransomware attempt, a deleted SharePoint library or a failed server does happen, recovery time is what separates an annoying morning from a lost week.
