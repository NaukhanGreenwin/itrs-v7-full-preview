Managed IT Services vs Break-Fix IT Support in North York

Break-fix IT support is hourly: something stops, you call, and you pay for the time it takes to fix it. For a small North York office with everything in Microsoft 365, no server and a handful of staff, that can be the sensible choice, and we offer it. One-off work is scoped in writing before it starts and billed by the hour.

Managed IT services are a flat monthly fee for an agreed scope, and the difference is what gets done when nobody has called. Under break-fix nobody is paid to patch machines, check that last night's backup ran or close the account of the employee who left in March, so those jobs slip. Under a managed agreement they are the work. The usual tipping point is when staff cannot work without their systems, when the business holds client or patient data it is obliged to protect, or when an insurer or a large client starts sending security questionnaires. We will tell you honestly which side of that line we think you are on.

Cybersecurity Basics for North York Businesses

Most incidents at North York offices are ordinary: a phishing email that captures a Microsoft 365 password, a look-alike domain used to redirect an invoice payment, a laptop that missed months of updates, or a former employee's account that still works. Each has a known control. Multi-factor authentication stops most stolen-password sign-ins. Email filtering and SPF, DKIM and DMARC on your domain make phishing and spoofing harder. Endpoint protection and scheduled patching close the gaps attackers rely on, and a written offboarding checklist makes sure access ends the day someone leaves.

Backups are the last line, and the question that matters is whether a restore has been tested, not whether a backup job exists; how we monitor, test and document that is set out on our page for backup and disaster recovery in North York and Toronto. For clinics in west North York, the same controls, together with access logging and encryption, support PHIPA obligations; for law and accounting firms near Yonge and Sheppard they support the confidentiality expectations clients and regulators already have. Every one of these controls is included in our managed agreement rather than sold as an add-on, and the detail is on our managed cybersecurity page.

Switching to a New IT Provider in North York: How Onboarding Works

Changing IT providers sounds riskier than it is, provided the work is done in order. Ours runs in four documented stages. Assess: we review your devices, network, Microsoft 365 tenant, backups and security controls, and confirm who holds each administrative account. Plan: you receive a written plan showing what changes, when and why, with anything urgent called out before full cutover. Onboard: we document the environment, deploy monitoring and security tooling, configure backups and multi-factor authentication and introduce your staff to the helpdesk. Operate: ongoing 24/7 support and proactive management with scheduled reviews.

The step that causes real trouble elsewhere is ownership. Before anything moves, confirm that your domain, DNS and Microsoft 365 tenant are registered to your business and that someone at your company holds global administrator. If they sit with an outgoing provider, a routine handover becomes a negotiation. We check this during the free assessment, and everything we set up stays in your business's name.

Cybersecurity Services in North York: Protecting Business Data

Business data security starts with knowing where the data actually lives. For most North York firms that is Microsoft 365 mailboxes and SharePoint, one or two line-of-business applications, laptops that leave the office every night and, sometimes, a server in a cupboard. Our cybersecurity services begin by mapping those places and who can reach each one, then closing the gaps in order: multi-factor authentication and conditional access on every account, encryption on every laptop, endpoint protection with monitoring behind it, and sharing settings that are deliberate rather than left at their defaults.

The second half is being able to recover and to account for what happened. Backups are kept out of reach of the live environment and restore-tested, and sign-in and audit logs are retained so an incident has a timeline. That matters legally as well as practically: under PIPEDA, an organisation must report a breach of security safeguards involving personal information to the Privacy Commissioner when it creates a real risk of significant harm, and keep a record of every breach. Clinics in west North York also answer to PHIPA. The full programme is described on our managed cybersecurity page.
