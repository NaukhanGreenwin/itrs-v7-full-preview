What happens when someone in your Burlington office calls the help desk

A help desk is judged on the first five minutes, so it is worth describing exactly what those minutes look like. Your staff reach us by phone at (289) 582-9930, by email or through the ticket portal. Every request is logged with a reference number the moment it arrives, so nothing lives only in someone's memory or a sticky note. It is then triaged by business impact rather than by arrival order: a reception PC that cannot open the scheduling system, or a production office that has lost its connection to the ERP, goes ahead of a request to add a shared calendar.

For most everyday problems the technician who picks up the ticket connects to the user's machine with their permission and fixes it while they are still on the line. Anything that needs deeper work moves to the engineer who owns that part of your environment, and the ticket keeps its reference and its history rather than being closed and reopened under a new number. The user is told what was done and, where it matters, why it happened, so the same call does not come back next week.

Because the help desk is part of a managed agreement, the technician answering already has your documentation: which line-of-business applications you run, who your internet provider is, where the backups go and which staff are allowed to approve changes. That is the practical difference between an IT help desk and a generic support line.

What the help desk handles day to day

The bulk of help desk work in a Burlington office is unglamorous and important: password resets and multi-factor authentication re-registration, Outlook and Teams problems, shared mailboxes and calendars, OneDrive and SharePoint permissions, printers and scanners that will not map, laptops that will not connect to the office Wi-Fi or the VPN, and the slow-computer complaints that are often a failing drive or a full disk in disguise.

Then there are the requests that keep a business running smoothly rather than fixing something broken. New starters get an account, a licence, a configured laptop and the right group memberships before their first morning. Leavers have their access removed the same day, their mailbox converted or delegated, and their devices wiped or reassigned. Line-of-business applications such as accounting, practice-management or dispatch software are supported directly where we can and through the vendor where we must, with us holding the call so your staff do not have to sit in two queues.

The tickets we care most about are the awkward ones: the intermittent fault nobody can reproduce, the application that fails only for one department, the problem that turns out to be the internet circuit rather than the software. Those are tracked to a written cause rather than closed on a reboot.

A help desk that does not become the way in

Help desks are a known target. An attacker who calls pretending to be a staff member locked out of their account, and talks a technician into resetting the password and multi-factor authentication, has bypassed every other control. So our technicians verify identity before any reset or MFA change, using a method agreed with your business in advance, and changes to finance or administrator accounts need confirmation from the person you have named as an approver. It is slightly slower on the rare genuine call and it closes one of the easiest attacks there is.

The same discipline applies to the rest of the job. Offboarding happens the day someone leaves, not when somebody remembers. Admin rights are given for a task and removed after it. Mailbox forwarding rules that send mail outside the company are flagged rather than ignored. And because the help desk sees email problems first, it is often where spoofing shows up: in our scan of 481 GTA business domains, 91.7% published SPF but only 20.6% enforced DMARC, so most domains could still be impersonated. Fixing that is part of the managed service, not an add-on, and the full set of controls is described on our page for cybersecurity services in Burlington.

Questions to ask any Burlington help desk provider

Whoever you choose, a few questions separate a real help desk from an answering service. Who picks up at 2 am on a Sunday, and can that person actually fix the problem or only take a message? Is after-hours support included in the monthly fee or billed per call? How are tickets prioritised when three come in at once? Will you get a monthly report showing what was raised, how long it took and what keeps recurring?

Two more matter later rather than now. Are the administrator accounts for your Microsoft 365 tenant, domain registration and firewall held in your company's name, so you can leave if you ever need to? And what does the provider need from your current one to take over cleanly? Our guide on how to switch IT providers lists the handover items, and it applies whether or not you choose us. The city-wide picture, including network support and managed IT, is on our IT support in Burlington page.
