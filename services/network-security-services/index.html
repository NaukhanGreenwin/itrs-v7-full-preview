What Our Network Security Services Include

Firewall management comes first: firmware kept current, every rule tied to a written reason and reviewed, administrative interfaces kept off the internet, and inbound services that nobody can justify closed. Intrusion detection and prevention runs on the firewall where the hardware supports it, so known attack patterns are blocked and logged rather than just passed through.

Inside the network, segmentation separates staff devices, guest Wi-Fi, phones, cameras, printers, servers and any production or building equipment, so one compromised device cannot reach everything else. Wireless is set up with current encryption and separate networks per purpose, and switch ports in public areas are not left live by default. Where an existing firewall can do the job we keep it; where it is out of vendor support we say so, because an unpatched security appliance is a liability however well it appears to run.

Around those sit secure VPN and remote access (covered below), vulnerability scanning of your internet-facing addresses and internal devices so missing patches and exposed services are found by us rather than by an attacker, and 24/7 monitoring of everything above. Each finding goes onto a written list with a fix and an owner, rather than into a report nobody reads.

Network Security Monitoring Services: What Gets Watched and Who Responds

Network security monitoring means someone is reading the signals, not just collecting them. We watch firewall and intrusion alerts, VPN and remote sign-ins, new or unknown devices joining the network, configuration changes on firewalls and switches, and traffic patterns that do not match how the business normally works, such as a workstation suddenly talking to servers it has never used.

Alerts reach a technician at any hour. When something needs action the response is containment first: isolating a device, disabling an account, blocking an address, then working out what happened and recording it. Because the same team runs your helpdesk and endpoints under our managed security services, the network alert and the laptop that caused it are investigated together rather than passed between vendors. When the question afterwards is what an attacker did inside a mailbox or cloud tenant, our digital forensics for Microsoft 365 and Google Workspace service preserves and documents the available evidence.

Remote Access, VPN and Zero Trust

Remote access is where most small-business networks are weakest: remote desktop exposed to the internet, a VPN with shared or password-only accounts, and remote-access accounts for people who left years ago. We close exposed remote desktop, put multi-factor authentication on every remote connection, match VPN accounts to the current staff list and remove access the day someone leaves.

Zero trust is the next step rather than a product to buy: every connection is checked on who the person is, whether their device is managed and healthy, and whether they need that specific application, instead of trusting anything that is on the office network. For many Toronto businesses that means Microsoft 365 conditional access and per-application access replacing a VPN that opened the whole network, phased in so staff are not locked out on day one.

Choosing a Network Security Provider

Most network security providers list the same products, so compare them on how the work is done. Ask who reads firewall and intrusion alerts at night and whether that person can act on them; whether every firewall rule is documented with a reason; whether the provider also runs the network itself or only the security layer, because split ownership is where changes break things; and whether you, not the provider, hold the administrative credentials for your own equipment.

Ask also what is inside the agreement and what is billed separately. Monitoring, rule reviews, firmware updates and vulnerability scanning are ongoing work; if any of them is an add-on, it is the part most likely to stop happening. A good provider will put all of this in writing before you sign, and will tell you plainly when existing equipment can stay.

Network Security Services FAQs

How do I choose a network security provider?

Compare how the work is done rather than the product list: who reads alerts at night and can act on them, whether firewall rules are documented and reviewed, whether the same team runs the network and its security, whether you keep the administrative credentials, and whether monitoring, firmware updates and vulnerability scanning are in the base agreement or billed separately.

What do network security monitoring services watch for?

Firewall and intrusion alerts, VPN and remote sign-ins, unknown devices joining the network, configuration changes on firewalls and switches, and traffic that does not match how the business normally works. Alerts reach a technician at any hour, and the response is containment first: isolating a device, disabling an account or blocking an address, then investigating.

How often should a business have a network security audit?

At least once a year, and again after any significant change: a new office or site, a firewall replacement, a move to new remote-access arrangements, or a change of IT provider. Managed clients get ongoing vulnerability scanning and rule reviews between audits, so the annual review confirms the state of the network rather than discovering it.

What is included in managed network security services?

Managed network security can include firewall configuration and maintenance, network segmentation, secure wired and wireless settings, access controls, patching, continuous monitoring, alert investigation, and remote or on-site response across Toronto and the GTA.

How is network security different from network management?

Network management focuses on reliable connectivity, performance, capacity, and device health. Network security focuses on controlling access, reducing exposure, monitoring suspicious activity, and containing threats. IT Rapid Support can manage both together so security does not come at the expense of reliability.

Can you secure an existing business network?

Yes. We can review the current firewall, switching, Wi-Fi, remote access, segmentation, and monitoring setup, identify practical gaps, and then manage the agreed controls without requiring a complete rebuild when the existing equipment can support them.

Do you provide 24/7 network security monitoring?

Yes. IT Rapid Support provides 24/7 monitoring and response as part of its managed cybersecurity services, backed by remote support and on-site service across the Greater Toronto Area when hands-on work is needed.

Do we need to replace our firewall to get managed network security?

Not necessarily. If the existing firewall is still supported by its vendor and receiving security updates, we bring its rules, firmware and logging up to standard and manage it. If it is past end of support, or cannot do intrusion prevention or segmentation, we recommend a replacement and explain why before anything is bought.

What is network segmentation and does a small office need it?

Segmentation splits one flat network into separate zones, for example staff, guests, phones, cameras, printers and servers, with rules about what can talk to what. Small offices benefit most, because they often have cameras, smart devices and guest Wi-Fi on the same network as the accounting files. It limits how far an attacker or a piece of ransomware can spread from the first device it reaches.

Do you offer a network security assessment in Toronto?

Yes, and the first one is free. It covers what your public IP addresses expose, the firewall rules and firmware, how the internal network is segmented, wireless configuration and who has remote access. You get the findings in writing, in priority order, whether or not you engage us to fix them.
