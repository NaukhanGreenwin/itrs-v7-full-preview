What Counts as an IT Emergency (and What Can Wait)

An IT emergency is a failure that stops people working, puts data at risk or is still spreading. A server that will not start, an office with no internet, email that has stopped going in or out, phones or Teams calling that have gone silent, a line-of-business application nobody can open, files being encrypted, or a mailbox sending messages nobody wrote: each of those is an emergency because every hour it continues costs money or widens the damage.

Some problems feel urgent but can usually wait for the next business day: one slow PC when others are working, a single printer, a new account for someone who starts next week. If you are not sure which side of the line you are on, call (289) 582-9930 anyway. We will tell you honestly if it can wait, and if it cannot, work starts on the call.

The First Hour of an IT Emergency

What happens in the first hour decides how long the whole outage lasts. On your side: write down what you saw and when, do not keep restarting a machine you think is compromised, unplug its network cable instead of powering it off, do not pay any ransom or reply to an attacker, and gather whatever admin logins you hold. Our IT emergency response checklist sets those steps out in order so someone in the office can follow it while you are on the phone with us.

On our side, the early part of the call follows a fixed order: establish what is down and who is affected, check for signs of a security incident, get remote access, contain anything that is spreading, and decide whether a technician needs to be on site. You get a plain update at each step, so you know what to tell staff and clients.

Ransomware, Server Down or Email Outage: How the Response Differs

Ransomware is handled as a security incident first and an outage second. Affected machines are isolated, the accounts an attacker could reuse are locked down, and evidence is preserved before anything is wiped, because your insurer or lawyer may need it. Recovery only starts once we know the backups are clean and the way in has been closed.

A server that is down is a hardware, storage or software question. Remote checks look at power, disk and storage health, the virtualisation host and any update that ran before the failure. If the hardware has failed, the priority is getting the business running again, from backup or a temporary host if needed, while the replacement is arranged.

An email outage is usually in the tenant or the domain rather than on anyone's PC: a Microsoft 365 service incident, an expired licence or domain, a changed DNS or MX record, a broken mail flow rule, or an account blocked from sending after a compromise. Those are fixed remotely, in the tenant or DNS, often without anyone needing to visit.

Emergency IT Support Services: What Happens When You Call

Every emergency follows the same discipline. First, triage: we establish what is down, what it is costing you, and whether there are signs of a security incident. Second, remote stabilization: most outages — stalled servers, broken email flow, misbehaving firewalls, locked-out accounts — can be diagnosed and often resolved remotely, and that work begins on the first call. Third, on-site response: if the fastest fix is physical, a technician is dispatched from Vaughan with the goal of getting you operating again before worrying about anything cosmetic.

Once you are back up, we do not just close the ticket. You get a plain-language explanation of what failed and why, and what would prevent it from happening again — whether that is fixing a failed backup job, replacing hardware running past its supported life, or closing the security gap that let an attacker in.

Emergency Cybersecurity Help: Ransomware, Hacked Email and Suspected Breaches

Emergency cybersecurity help starts with containment, not cleanup. If you see encrypted files, a ransom note, strange sign-ins or messages sent from your account that nobody wrote, disconnect the affected machines from the network without powering them off, do not pay anything, and call (289) 582-9930. On the call we work out what is affected, isolate it, and lock down the accounts an attacker could use to come back.

From there the work follows our cyber incident response process: find out how they got in and how far they spread, restore from clean backups, and keep the evidence your insurer or lawyer may need. Businesses that want this watched around the clock afterwards can add managed threat detection and response, so the next attack is caught while it is still small.

Emergency IT Support for Common Outages: Servers, Internet, Email and Phones

Most emergency IT support calls fall into a few patterns. A server will not boot or a shared drive has vanished. The office internet is down, or it is up but nothing can reach the line-of-business application. Email has stopped flowing in or out. Phones or Teams calling have gone quiet. A firewall update or a failed disk has taken half the network with it.

Each has a short list of likely causes, and remote checks narrow it down in minutes rather than hours: is it the hardware, the connection, the licence, the configuration or the provider? When the cause is a failed Microsoft 365 setting or licence, our Microsoft 365 managed services team fixes it in the tenant directly. When the cause is physical, a technician comes on site with a clear idea of what to bring.

Incident Management: How We Run an IT Emergency From First Call to Written Report

Incident management is the part of emergency IT that stops a bad day becoming a bad week. One person owns the incident from the first call, keeps a running timeline of what was found and done, and gives you plain updates on what is working, what is not and what comes next, so you can decide what to tell staff and clients.

Work is done in a fixed order: stop the damage, restore the systems the business needs first, then restore everything else, then fix the root cause. If backups are part of the recovery, we restore in the order your business needs rather than the order the files happen to be stored. Our business continuity and disaster recovery service is how that order gets written down before the next emergency. For the steps your own staff can take in the first hour, before anyone arrives, see our IT emergency first-hour checklist.

Emergency IT Services FAQs

What are emergency IT services?

Emergency IT services are urgent IT support for a business that cannot work because of a technology failure or a cyber incident, such as a server or network outage, email that has stopped, or ransomware. IT Rapid Support answers emergency calls 24/7 at (289) 582-9930, starts remote diagnosis on the call and sends a technician on site from Vaughan when the fix needs hands on hardware.

Do we need a contract before you start emergency IT work?

No. Businesses without an agreement can call and we start on the problem. Emergency work is billed by the hour, we tell you what we are going to do before we do it, and any larger recovery work is scoped in writing before it starts. There is no obligation to sign up for anything afterwards.

What should we have ready when we call for emergency IT support?

A short description of what stopped working and when, who is affected, anything that changed recently, and any admin access you hold, such as the Microsoft 365 global admin account, the firewall login and the backup console. If you do not have those, call anyway; we work around missing access and help you recover it afterwards.

Do you provide emergency IT support to businesses that are not existing clients?

Yes. Call (289) 582-9930 and we will triage the issue right away. We help new businesses through emergencies — server outages, network failures, email down, or cyber incidents — and many stay on afterwards as managed IT clients so problems are prevented rather than repaired.

How fast can you respond to an IT emergency in Toronto or the GTA?

Remote support starts on the first call — our 24/7 helpdesk begins diagnosing the moment you reach us. If on-site help is required, we dispatch certified technicians from our Vaughan office across Toronto and the GTA, prioritized by severity.

What counts as an IT emergency?

Anything stopping your business from operating: servers or networks down, email outages, phones offline, critical applications failing, suspected ransomware or account compromise, or data loss. If revenue or safety is affected, treat it as an emergency and call — we will tell you honestly what it needs.

What should we do first if we suspect ransomware or a breach?

Disconnect affected machines from the network — do not power them off or pay anything — and call us at (289) 582-9930. We isolate the incident, assess the spread, and work to restore from clean backups while preserving evidence.

How is emergency IT support billed if we are not a client?

Emergency work for businesses without an agreement is billed by the hour, and we tell you what we are doing before we do it. Managed IT clients are covered under their agreement.

Is emergency IT support available on weekends and holidays?

Yes. The helpdesk runs 24/7, including nights, weekends and statutory holidays, so an outage on a Saturday night or over a long weekend is picked up when you call rather than on the next business day.

Our current IT provider is not answering. Can you step in?

Yes. Call (289) 582-9930 and we will start on the problem. If you can, have whatever admin credentials you hold ready, such as the Microsoft 365 global admin, the firewall and the backup console. If you do not have them, we will work around it and help you recover them afterwards.

Can you help if our Microsoft 365 email account has been hacked?

Yes. Call (289) 582-9930. We sign the attacker out, reset the password and multi-factor authentication, remove forwarding and inbox rules they added, check what was sent from the mailbox and what else the account could reach, and help you warn anyone who received fraudulent messages. Then we close the gap that let them in.

Do you provide emergency IT support outside Toronto?

Yes. Emergency calls are taken from businesses across the GTA, including York Region, Peel, Halton and Durham. Remote work starts on the call wherever you are; on-site help is dispatched from our Vaughan office when the fix needs hands on hardware, and travel time depends on where you are and the time of day.

Will we get a written report after an emergency?

Yes. Once you are working again we write up what failed, what we did, what was affected and what we recommend so it does not happen again. If an insurer, client or regulator may ask questions, tell us early so we keep the timeline and logs they will want to see.
