IT continuity services: business continuity planning for Toronto businesses

Disaster recovery brings the technology back. Business continuity is the wider plan for how the business keeps working while it comes back: who can work from home on Microsoft 365 if the office is closed, how phones are redirected, which suppliers and clients are told, and which manual workarounds keep orders, payroll or appointments moving for a day or two. IT continuity services connect the two, so the IT recovery order matches the order the business actually needs.

In practice we work through it with the owner or office manager rather than handing over a template. We list the processes that cannot stop, the systems and people each one depends on, and the realistic disruptions for an office in Toronto or North York: a server failure, ransomware on a shared drive, a building closure, an internet outage or a key person being unavailable. The result is a short document with contacts, decisions already made and the steps for each scenario, reviewed when the business or its systems change.

Recovery testing, RTO and RPO explained plainly

Recovery time objective (RTO) is how long a system can be down before the damage becomes serious. Recovery point objective (RPO) is how much recent work you can afford to lose, measured in time: if the last good copy is from last night, everything done today is gone. Each important system gets its own two numbers, chosen by the business, and those numbers decide how often it is backed up and how recovery is designed. A file share that changes all day needs a shorter RPO than an archive that changes once a month.

Testing is how you find out whether those targets are realistic. We restore files, mailboxes and, where the design allows, whole systems into an isolated location so staff are not disrupted, then record what was restored, how long it took and anything that did not work. When a test shows the agreed target cannot be met with the current setup, you get a written explanation of the gap and the options for closing it, and the decision stays with you.

IT disaster recovery services vs backup alone: what to ask a provider

Many businesses already pay for a backup product and assume that is disaster recovery. The difference shows up on the bad day. Questions worth asking any provider: Is there a copy the office network cannot reach or delete? Who reads the backup reports, and what happens when a job fails? When was the last test restore, and is there a written record of it? Is Microsoft 365 data backed up separately? Does a written plan say what is restored first and who makes the calls?

If the honest answer to most of those is no, the business has copies of data but no tested way back. That is the gap IT disaster recovery services are meant to close, and it is usually cheaper to close it before an incident than during one. Our managed security services work alongside backup and recovery, because ransomware recovery depends on both stopping the attacker and restoring clean data.

How onboarding works: from first review to a tested plan

It starts with what you already have. We list the systems the business depends on, where the data lives, what is currently backed up, how often, and when anyone last restored from it. That review regularly finds gaps such as a server nobody included, Microsoft 365 data with no separate backup, or a backup that has been failing quietly for months.

Next, you decide two numbers for each important system: how much recent data you can afford to lose and how long you can afford to be down. Those are business decisions, and they set the backup frequency and the design. We then deploy or correct the backups, run a first test restore, and write the recovery plan with you. Business Continuity & Disaster Recovery FAQs

What do backup and disaster recovery services in Toronto include?

With IT Rapid Support they include automated local and cloud backups of servers, selected workstations and Microsoft 365 data, a copy kept out of reach of the office network, daily monitoring of backup jobs, scheduled and recorded test restores, a written recovery plan with agreed RTO and RPO targets, and 24/7 support to carry out that plan when systems go down. The service is delivered across Toronto, North York and the GTA from our Vaughan office.

What are IT continuity services?

IT continuity services make sure the technology a business depends on keeps working, or comes back in a planned order, during a disruption such as ransomware, a server failure, an internet outage or an office closure. They combine backup and disaster recovery with continuity planning: remote-work arrangements, contact lists, decisions made in advance and documented steps for each likely scenario.

Can disaster recovery be tested without disrupting staff?

Usually, yes. Most restore tests are run into an isolated location, so files, mailboxes and systems can be recovered and checked without touching the live environment. We record what was restored and how long it took. Tests that need a system taken offline, such as a full failover, are scheduled with you in advance, often outside business hours.

What is the difference between business continuity and disaster recovery?

Business continuity is the broader plan for keeping essential operations moving during a disruption. Disaster recovery focuses on restoring data, systems, applications, and technology services. A resilient business needs both the operating plan and the technical recovery capability.

Is having cloud backup enough for disaster recovery?

No. Backup provides copies of data, while disaster recovery defines what gets restored, in what order, by whom, and within which recovery targets. The backups also need monitoring and restore testing so the business knows they can be used when needed.

What do RTO and RPO mean?

Recovery Time Objective (RTO) is the maximum acceptable downtime for a system. Recovery Point Objective (RPO) is the amount of recent data the business can tolerate losing. We use these targets to shape backup frequency, recovery priorities, and the recovery plan.

Can IT Rapid Support help test an existing recovery plan?

Yes. We can review the systems and dependencies covered by the plan, verify backup and recovery procedures, run restore tests, and help update the runbook so responsibilities, contacts, and recovery steps reflect the current environment.

Do you provide backup and disaster recovery for businesses in Toronto, North York and York Region?

Yes. Backup monitoring, restore testing and most recovery work are done remotely for businesses across Toronto, North York, York Region and the wider GTA, and technicians are dispatched from our Vaughan office when recovery needs hands on hardware.

How often should a business test its backups?

Often enough that you would notice a problem before you need the backup. We run scheduled test restores and record the date and what was restored, and we also test after major changes such as a new server or a migration. A backup that has never been restored from should be treated as unproven.

Does Microsoft 365 need its own backup?

Yes. Microsoft keeps the service running, but deleted or encrypted mail and files are only recoverable for limited periods under its retention settings. A separate backup of Exchange Online, OneDrive, SharePoint and Teams data lets you restore from a known point after ransomware, an accidental deletion or a departing employee clearing a mailbox.
