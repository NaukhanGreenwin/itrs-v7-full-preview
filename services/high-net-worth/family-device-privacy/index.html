Every phone, tablet and laptop in the household set up properly, kept current and handed back cleanly.

In detail

Household staff are the gap most families miss

House managers, assistants, nannies, drivers and housekeepers often need calendars, cameras, door codes, delivery accounts and sometimes a family card. The easy route is to share a family member's login or a common password, and that access tends to outlast the job.

We give staff their own accounts and, where it makes sense, a household-owned phone, with access limited to what the role needs. When someone leaves we follow a written checklist: accounts closed, door and Wi-Fi codes changed, shared vault items rotated, and the device wiped and returned. The family also gets a plain record of who has access to what, which is the first thing to check if something goes missing.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

How do you manage children's devices without being intrusive?

Parents set the rules and we put them in place: screen time, app approval, content filters and location sharing, using the controls built into Apple and Google devices. Settings are loosened as children get older.

Should household staff use family devices or accounts?

No. Give staff their own accounts and, where needed, a household-owned device. Access can then be limited to the job and removed cleanly without changing the family's own passwords.

What happens to a device when a staff member leaves?

If the household owns it, it is returned, wiped and reissued. If it is the employee's own phone, we remove household accounts, apps and access from it. Either way, door codes, alarm codes and Wi-Fi passwords they knew are changed.

Can you back up family photos and documents?

Yes. We set up backups for phones and computers, check that they actually complete, and make sure important photos and documents are not stored in only one place.
