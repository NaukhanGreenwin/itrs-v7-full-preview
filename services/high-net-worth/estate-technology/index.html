In detail

Why technology in large homes goes wrong

Most problems in large homes are not one failed device. The builder's low-voltage contractor, the AV integrator, the alarm company and the internet provider each put in their own equipment, often their own router, and nobody owns how it fits together. Wi-Fi drops between floors, cameras stop recording without anyone noticing, and the passwords stay with whichever installer set them up.

We start with a walk-through and an inventory, then design one network with proper access points, wired backhaul where it matters and separate segments for family, guests, staff and building systems. Everything is written down, admin credentials move into the household's own vault, and the equipment is monitored, so a failure is usually picked up before the family has to report it.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

Do you install audio-visual or home automation systems?

We are not an AV or automation dealer. We build and run the network those systems rely on, secure them, and work with your integrator, who stays responsible for their equipment and programming.

We are building or renovating. When should you get involved?

Before the walls close. Cable runs, access point locations, a ventilated network cabinet with clean power, and conduit to outbuildings are cheap during construction and expensive afterwards. We can review drawings with your builder and low-voltage contractor.

Who should hold the passwords to our home systems?

The household should. Admin credentials for the network, cameras and building systems go into a password vault you control, shared with us and with installers as needed. No one person's phone should be the only place a password lives.

Can you look after the household's computers and phones as well?

Yes. Family and staff devices are set up, updated and backed up under the same arrangement, as described on our family device and privacy page.
