In detail

Why smart homes feel unreliable

When a scene does not run or the lights stop responding, the cause is usually the network: a consumer router overloaded by dozens of devices, Wi-Fi that does not reach the garage or the dock, or an internet outage that takes cloud-dependent devices with it. The automation gets blamed for a network problem.

Security has the same root. Cameras, doorbells, TVs and hubs often ship with weak defaults and stop getting updates after a few years. We put them on a separate network that cannot reach the family's computers, change default credentials, turn off remote features nobody uses, and keep a list of devices due for replacement once their maker stops supporting them.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

Do you program systems like Control4 or Crestron?

No. Those systems are programmed by their authorised dealers. We build and support the network they run on, secure the devices, and work with your integrator so each problem is fixed by whoever owns it.

Will our smart home keep working when the internet is down?

Partly. Devices and platforms that support local control keep working, and a backup internet connection, such as cellular or Starlink, keeps cameras and remote access up. We tell you which devices depend on the cloud before you rely on them.

Are smart home devices a security risk?

They can be. Cheap or unsupported devices are a common way into home networks. Keeping them on their own network, updated and with unique passwords, limits what an attacker can reach even if one is compromised.

Do you support the cottage as well as the main home?

Yes. Many families run the same platforms in the city and at the cottage. Our cottage IT and Starlink pages cover remote properties.
