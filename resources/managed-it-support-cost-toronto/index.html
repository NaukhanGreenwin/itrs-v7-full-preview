The Main Managed IT Pricing Models

Per-user pricing

The most common model for small and mid-sized Canadian businesses is a flat monthly fee for each person who uses technology. It covers the devices and accounts that person uses, it is predictable, and it scales cleanly as you hire or shrink. It works best where each person has a laptop, a phone and a set of cloud apps. The thing to check is who counts as a user: a provider that counts only full-time office staff and one that counts every mailbox, contractor and shared account can quote the same per-user rate and land far apart.

Per-device pricing

Some providers price each managed device instead: workstations, servers, firewalls, switches and sometimes printers. This suits businesses with shared workstations or a high device-to-person ratio, such as warehouses, clinics, retail stores and shop floors. Servers and network equipment are often priced at a different rate from workstations because they carry more monitoring and patching work. Our managed IT plans page explains that we price per user or per device depending on which fits the business.

Tiered plans

Most managed service providers package the work into tiers, where each tier is a different scope rather than the same service at three prices. IT Rapid Support publishes three: Co-Managed IT, Fully Managed IT, and Managed IT + Security. The line-by-line inclusion matrix is on the managed IT plans page; the short version is that Fully Managed IT adds an unlimited remote help desk, 24/7 monitoring, backups with tested restores, onboarding and offboarding and a quarterly technology review on top of co-managed support, and Managed IT + Security adds 24/7 threat detection and response, enforced MFA, SPF, DKIM and DMARC hardening, awareness training and incident response planning.

Co-managed pricing

Co-managed IT is priced for businesses that already have an internal IT person or team. The provider supplies what the internal team lacks, typically overflow and after-hours help desk cover, tooling such as patching and endpoint protection, backup monitoring and an escalation path to senior engineers, and the fee reflects that narrower scope. The key is a written split of responsibilities, so neither side assumes the other is watching the backups. Our guide to co-managed versus fully managed IT explains why the two are priced differently, and the service itself is described on our co-managed IT services page.

Hourly and block-of-hours pricing

Hourly billing, sometimes sold as a prepaid block of hours, is the break-fix model: you pay when something is fixed. It suits very small offices with simple setups, or one-off work like an assessment or a migration. The weakness is structural. Nobody is paid to prevent problems, monitoring and patching tend to happen only when someone remembers, and the bill is highest in exactly the month the business can least afford it. What Actually Drives the Monthly Price

Whatever the model, the same handful of inputs moves the number up or down.

  • Headcount and devices. More people and endpoints mean more to support, monitor, patch and secure. This is usually the single largest input.
  • Sites and servers. One office in Vaughan is a different job from four locations with on-premises servers between them. Locations, servers and network sites move the price independently of headcount.
  • Scope tier. Help desk only, fully managed, or managed plus security operations are different amounts of ongoing work.
  • Security and compliance. A business handling client files, health information or payment data needs more layers and more evidence: endpoint detection and response, email authentication, MFA everywhere, awareness training and reporting that supports PIPEDA or PHIPA obligations.
  • Coverage hours. A help desk staffed by engineers after hours costs more to run than business-hours support, and an answering service that opens a ticket for the morning is not the same product.
  • Starting condition. An undocumented environment with unknown admin accounts, untested backups and ageing servers takes onboarding work before it can be managed. Some providers charge this as a one-time onboarding fee and some absorb it; either way, ask.
  • What you already have. If you already pay for a tool the provider would otherwise supply, or your own staff handle part of the work, that should come out of the scope.

What Is Usually Excluded From a Managed IT Fee

Most disputes about managed IT cost are really disputes about scope. These items are commonly outside the monthly fee, so get a written answer on each before you sign.

  • Projects. Office moves, server replacements, Microsoft 365 migrations and new-site builds are typically quoted separately. A live example: replacing or migrating a Windows Server 2016 machine before its end of support on January 12, 2027 is project work under most agreements, not routine support.
  • Hardware and software licences. Laptops, firewalls, Microsoft 365 licences and line-of-business software are usually purchased separately, sometimes resold by the provider.
  • On-site visits. Included, included up to a limit, or billed per visit, and is travel time charged?
  • After-hours work. Is after-hours support part of the plan, or billed at a different rate?
  • Third-party systems. Phone systems, printers, vendor-managed equipment and specialist industry software often sit outside scope or are supported best-effort.
  • Security operations. Endpoint protection may be included while 24/7 threat detection and response, awareness training or compliance reporting are add-ons. Our guide to managed IT versus managed security explains why these are different products.

None of these exclusions is wrong in itself. The problem is discovering them on the first invoice rather than in the proposal.

How vCIO Services Are Priced

Virtual CIO (vCIO) work, meaning technology planning, budgeting, lifecycle tracking and regular business reviews, is priced in one of three ways. It can be bundled into a managed IT agreement, sold as a separate monthly retainer, or bought as a one-off engagement such as a roadmap or assessment. On IT Rapid Support's Fully Managed IT and Managed IT + Security plans, a quarterly technology review and roadmap is part of the agreement rather than a separate line item; standalone strategy work is scoped in writing first. What drives the fee is the size and complexity of the environment, the number of locations, compliance obligations and how often you want formal reviews. When comparing vCIO quotes, ask what the deliverables are: a written roadmap, a budget, a lifecycle register and a risk register are real outputs; a quarterly slide deck is not. Our vCIO services guide and vCIO and IT strategy service cover what to expect.

How MDR and 24/7 Threat Detection Are Priced

Managed detection and response (MDR) is usually priced per protected endpoint or per user each month, either as a line item on its own or bundled into a security tier. The cost is driven less by the software and more by the people: someone has to watch the alerts around the clock, investigate them and act, for example isolating a compromised laptop at 3 a.m. When comparing MDR quotes, ask who responds and when, whether response actions are included or billed as incident response, whether Microsoft 365 identities and email are monitored as well as endpoints, and what the escalation path to you looks like. At IT Rapid Support, 24/7 threat detection and response is part of the Managed IT + Security plan rather than a separate contract. Our managed threat detection and MDR guide explains what the service should include.

Outsourced IT Cost Versus Hiring In-House

Outsourcing IT is often compared against one internal hire. The comparison is not just salary against a monthly fee: one person cannot cover 24/7, holidays, sick days and every specialty from networking to security, and the business still pays for tools, training and backup cover. Outsourcing also means different things to different providers, from helpdesk-only to full responsibility for the environment. Our IT outsourcing services guide explains the three shapes outsourced IT takes, and our managed IT versus in-house comparison works through the coverage maths.

How to Compare Managed IT Quotes

Two quotes are only comparable once both cover the same scope. Before comparing prices, put each proposal through the same questions:

  • Who is counted: users, devices, mailboxes, contractors, servers?
  • Is the help desk unlimited, or capped by hours or tickets, and what happens after the cap?
  • What does 24/7 mean: engineers who can fix things, or an answering service?
  • Are backups monitored and restore-tested, and when did the last test restore run?
  • Which security controls are in the base price and which are add-ons?
  • Are onboarding, projects, on-site visits, after-hours work, hardware and licences inside or outside the fee?
  • Who owns your Microsoft 365 tenant, domain and backup data, and what are the exit terms?

Frequently Asked Questions

How much do managed IT services cost in Canada?

Managed IT in Canada is usually a flat monthly fee charged per user or per device, and the rate depends on the scope (help desk only, fully managed, or managed plus security), the number of sites and servers, and your security and compliance needs. Because those inputs vary so much, a reliable figure needs an assessment of your environment. IT Rapid Support quotes an exact monthly figure after a free assessment, and one-off work is scoped and quoted in writing before it starts.

Is managed IT priced per user or per device?

Both models are common. Per-user pricing suits offices where each person has their own devices and cloud apps; per-device pricing suits shared workstations, warehouses, clinics and retail. Servers and network equipment are often priced separately from workstations. Ask any provider exactly who or what is being counted, because that is where similar-looking quotes diverge.

What is usually not included in a managed IT contract?

Projects such as migrations, office moves and server replacements, hardware and software licences, and sometimes on-site visits, after-hours work, third-party systems and advanced security operations. None of that is unusual, but it should be written into the proposal so the first invoice is not the first time you hear about it.

How is vCIO or IT strategy work priced?

It is either bundled into a managed IT agreement, sold as a monthly retainer, or bought as a one-off project such as a roadmap. On IT Rapid Support's Fully Managed IT and Managed IT + Security plans, a quarterly technology review and roadmap is included. Judge any vCIO quote by its written deliverables: roadmap, budget, lifecycle register and risk register.

How is MDR priced for Canadian small businesses?

Managed detection and response is usually priced per endpoint or per user per month, either standalone or bundled into a security tier. The main cost is the 24/7 people who investigate and respond to alerts, so ask who responds, how fast, and whether response actions are included. At IT Rapid Support, 24/7 threat detection and response is part of the Managed IT + Security plan.
