Managed IT Services Pricing in Canada (2026): Toronto & GTA Cost Guide

How managed IT is priced in Canada and Toronto in 2026: per-user, per-device, tiered, co-managed and hourly models, what drives cost, exclusions, vCIO and MDR.

Talk to us (289) 582-9930

Managed IT services in Canada are almost always priced as a flat monthly fee, calculated per user or per device, with the rate set by how much the provider is responsible for: helpdesk only, full outsourced IT, or full IT plus security operations.

The Main Managed IT Pricing Models

  • Per-user pricing: The most common model for small and mid-sized Canadian businesses is a flat monthly fee for each person who uses technology.
  • Per-device pricing: Some providers price each managed device instead: workstations, servers, firewalls, switches and sometimes printers.
  • Tiered plans: Most managed service providers package the work into tiers, where each tier is a different scope rather than the same service at three prices.
  • Co-managed pricing: Co-managed IT is priced for businesses that already have an internal IT person or team.
  • Hourly and block-of-hours pricing: Hourly billing, sometimes sold as a prepaid block of hours, is the break-fix model: you pay when something is fixed.

What Actually Drives the Monthly Price

Whatever the model, the same handful of inputs moves the number up or down.

  • Headcount and devices. More people and endpoints mean more to support, monitor, patch and secure. This is usually the single largest input.
  • Sites and servers: One office in Vaughan is a different job from four locations with on-premises servers between them.
  • Scope tier. Help desk only, fully managed, or managed plus security operations are different amounts of ongoing work.
  • Security and compliance: A business handling client files, health information or payment data needs more layers and more evidence: endpoint detection and response, email authentication, MFA everywhere, awareness training and reporting that supports PIPEDA or PHIPA obligations.
  • Coverage hours: A help desk staffed by engineers after hours costs more to run than business-hours support, and an answering service that opens a ticket for the morning is not the same product.

What Is Usually Excluded From a Managed IT Fee

Most disputes about managed IT cost are really disputes about scope. These items are commonly outside the monthly fee, so get a written answer on each before you sign.

  • Projects: Office moves, server replacements, Microsoft 365 migrations and new-site builds are typically quoted separately.
  • Hardware and software licences. Laptops, firewalls, Microsoft 365 licences and line-of-business software are usually purchased separately, sometimes resold by the provider.
  • On-site visits. Included, included up to a limit, or billed per visit, and is travel time charged?
  • After-hours work. Is after-hours support part of the plan, or billed at a different rate?
  • Third-party systems. Phone systems, printers, vendor-managed equipment and specialist industry software often sit outside scope or are supported best-effort.

None of these exclusions is wrong in itself. The problem is discovering them on the first invoice rather than in the proposal.

How vCIO Services Are Priced

Virtual CIO (vCIO) work, meaning technology planning, budgeting, lifecycle tracking and regular business reviews, is priced in one of three ways.

  • It can be bundled into a managed IT agreement, sold as a separate monthly retainer, or bought as a one-off engagement such as a roadmap or assessment.
  • On IT Rapid Support's Fully Managed IT and Managed IT + Security plans, a quarterly technology review and roadmap is part of the agreement rather than a separate line item.
  • What drives the fee is the size and complexity of the environment, the number of locations, compliance obligations and how often you want formal reviews.

How MDR and 24/7 Threat Detection Are Priced

Managed detection and response (MDR) is usually priced per protected endpoint or per user each month, either as a line item on its own or bundled into a security tier.

Outsourced IT Cost Versus Hiring In-House

Outsourcing IT is often compared against one internal hire. The comparison is not just salary against a monthly fee: one person cannot cover 24/7, holidays, sick days and every specialty from networking to security, and the business still pays for tools, training and backup cover.

Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

How much do managed IT services cost in Canada?

Managed IT in Canada is usually a flat monthly fee charged per user or per device, and the rate depends on the scope (help desk only, fully managed, or managed plus security), the number of sites and servers, and your security and compliance needs.

Is managed IT priced per user or per device?

Both models are common. Per-user pricing suits offices where each person has their own devices and cloud apps; per-device pricing suits shared workstations, warehouses, clinics and retail. Servers and network equipment are often priced separately from workstations.

What is usually not included in a managed IT contract?

Projects such as migrations, office moves and server replacements, hardware and software licences, and sometimes on-site visits, after-hours work, third-party systems and advanced security operations. None of that is unusual, but it should be written into the proposal so the first invoice is not the first time you hear about it.

How is vCIO or IT strategy work priced?

It is either bundled into a managed IT agreement, sold as a monthly retainer, or bought as a one-off project such as a roadmap. On IT Rapid Support's Fully Managed IT and Managed IT + Security plans, a quarterly technology review and roadmap is included.

How is MDR priced for Canadian small businesses?

Managed detection and response is usually priced per endpoint or per user per month, either standalone or bundled into a security tier. The main cost is the 24/7 people who investigate and respond to alerts, so ask who responds, how fast, and whether response actions are included.
