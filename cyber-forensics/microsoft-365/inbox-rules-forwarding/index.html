Microsoft 365 forensics

Inbox Rule and Mail Forwarding Forensics

Attackers hide inside a mailbox with rules. We show when each rule was made and what it did.

24/7 (289) 582-9930Report an incident

After a mailbox is compromised, attackers usually add inbox rules that move replies to an obscure folder, or forwarding that copies mail out of the tenant. We recover every rule and forwarding setting, date it from the audit log, and list the messages it touched.

What we do

What the investigation covers

  • Rule inventory

    Visible and hidden inbox rules exported with their conditions and actions.

  • Creation history

    New-InboxRule, Set-InboxRule and UpdateInboxRules events with IP and client.

  • Forwarding

    Mailbox forwarding, transport rules and external redirects found and dated.

  • Affected mail

    Messages moved, deleted or forwarded by each rule, identified by trace.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

What does a malicious inbox rule look like?

Typical rules have a one-character name such as a full stop, match words like invoice, payment or the targeted contact, and move or delete matching mail. Many move messages to RSS Feeds or Archive so the real user never sees replies.

Can a rule be hidden from Outlook?

Yes. Rules can be altered so they no longer appear in Outlook or the standard PowerShell output. We check the mailbox at a lower level and compare the audit log with what the client shows.

How far back can you see when forwarding was set up?

That depends on audit retention for your licence, commonly 180 days for standard audit and longer with premium audit. Current settings can always be read; history before the retention window cannot be recovered from Microsoft.

Should we delete the malicious rules straight away?

Export them first, then remove them. The audit log keeps the creation event, but the exact conditions and target folder are easiest to prove from an export taken before the rule is deleted.
