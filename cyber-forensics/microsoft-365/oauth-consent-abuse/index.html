Microsoft 365 forensics

Malicious OAuth App and Consent Grant Investigation

An app a user consented to keeps its access after the password is changed. We find it and close it.

24/7 (289) 582-9930Report an incident

In a consent phishing attack the user is asked to approve an app, and the app receives delegated permissions such as Mail.Read or Files.ReadWrite with an offline token. We identify every risky grant in the tenant, what the app did with it, and remove it without destroying the evidence.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

Why does a password reset not remove a malicious app?

The app holds its own refresh token issued under the user's consent. Until the permission grant is revoked and the service principal removed or disabled, the app can keep reading mail or files.

How do we know if an app was granted consent?

Entra ID audit logs record "Consent to application" and "Add delegated permission grant" events. We list every grant in the tenant and compare it with the apps the business actually uses.

What permissions are the most dangerous?

Mail.Read, Mail.Send, Files.ReadWrite.All and offline_access are common in consent phishing, because together they allow reading and sending mail without the user signed in. Application permissions granted by an admin carry even wider reach.

Should we stop users from consenting to apps?

For most organisations, yes. Allow user consent only for verified publishers and low-risk permissions, or turn it off and use the admin consent workflow so a reviewer approves each new app.
