Microsoft 365 forensics

Business Email Compromise Investigation

Find out how the mailbox was taken, what the attacker read and sent, and who else was targeted.

24/7 (289) 582-9930Report an incident

We investigate business email compromise in Microsoft 365 for organisations in Toronto and across the GTA, usually after a redirected payment or a warning from a client. The work answers three questions in writing: how access was gained, what the attacker did inside the mailbox, and whether the fraud started on your side or a supplier's.

What we do

What the investigation covers

  • Entry point

    Sign-in records, MFA events and phishing messages traced back to the first access.

  • Mailbox activity

    Messages read, sent, deleted and searched, from mailbox audit records.

  • Rules and forwarding

    Inbox rules, forwarding and delegates the attacker added to hide replies.

  • Whose side

    Message headers and trace show whether your tenant or the supplier's was used.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

What should we do in the first hour of a business email compromise?

Call your bank about any payment that left in the last few days, then revoke the user's sessions and reset the password. Do not delete the suspicious rules or messages yet: export or screenshot them first, because they are evidence of what happened.

Can you tell which emails the attacker read?

Often, yes. Microsoft 365 records mailbox access in the MailItemsAccessed audit event where the tenant's licence and audit settings capture it. If those records were not kept, we say so and work from sign-in, rule and message trace evidence instead.

How do you know whether our mailbox or the supplier's was compromised?

We compare the fraudulent message's headers, sending IP and authentication results with your message trace and sign-in logs. A lookalike domain, a message sent from your own mailbox and a message from the real supplier domain each point to a different place.

Should we report the fraud?

Report payment fraud to your bank, the police and the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre. If personal information may have been exposed, ask counsel whether a breach report to the Privacy Commissioner is required.
